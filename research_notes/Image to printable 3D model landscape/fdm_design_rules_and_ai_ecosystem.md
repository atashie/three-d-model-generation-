## Part A, Q1: Numeric FDM design rules (overhang, bridging, walls, features, text, holes, fits, chamfers, elephant foot, warping, anisotropy, TPU)

### Takeaway
Authoritative sources agree on the shape of the rules but not the numbers: unsupported overhang limits run 45° (generic) to 55–75° (modern, well-cooled printers); bridging limits run 5 mm (conservative service-bureau rule) to 30–40 mm (Bambu filament datasheets); minimum walls run 0.45 mm (one perimeter) to 1.2–1.6 mm (service bureaus). Encode each rule as a range with a "conservative" and "tuned-printer" value and attribute the source.

### Cited Findings

**Overhang angle**
- [P] "A 3D printer can cleanly print overhanging structures with an angle between 45 and 60 degrees" depending on nozzle and settings; Nextruder + 360° cooling models (MK4S, CORE One/+, CORE One L) "can print overhangs up to 75° without supports"; for a 0.25 mm nozzle "the angle might be less than 45 degrees" — [Prusa KB, Modeling with 3D printing in mind](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)
- [P] Overhangs "can usually be printed up to 45° without compromising quality"; at 45° the new layer rests on about 50% of the layer below — [Hubs, How to design parts for FDM](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-fdm-3d-printing/)
- [P] Prusa defines the overhang threshold as the most horizontal slope printable without support, measured from the horizontal plane (90° = vertical) — [Prusa KB, Failing supports](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/failing-supports_1807) (seen via search snippet)
- [P] Xometry FDM quick guide: "Unsupported angle from vertical 45°"; "Max unsupported overhang size 2 mm"; overhangs longer than 5 mm should be supported even if under 2 mm wide — [Xometry Pro, Quick Design Guide for FDM](https://xometry.pro/en/?p=154421)
- [S] Bambu Lab PLA Basic technical data sheet lists "Max Overhang Angle 55°, Max Bridging Length 30 mm"; engineering filaments (ABS-GF, PC, PA6-GF, PPA-CF, ASA-CF, ASA Aero, PPS-CF) list ~70° and ~40 mm; PET-CF ~70° and ~30 mm — [Bambu PLA Basic TDS PDF](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/filament-acc/abs-asa-pc/bambu_pla_basic_technical_data_sheet.pdf), [ABS-GF TDS](https://wiki.bambulab.com/filament-acc/absgf-pa6gf/2450f3fe0251448c91c5a191b7cb6a1e.pdf) (direct fetch blocked; values from search snippets)
- [S] Markforged Composites Design Guide: minimum unsupported overhang angle 40°, Eiger generates supports below 45°; Onyx needs support above 45° from vertical, nylon above 30° from vertical — [Markforged Composites Design Guide PDF](https://static.markforged.com/downloads/CompositesDesignGuide.pdf), [Markforged, Supports and 3D printing](https://support.markforged.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000724710-Supports-and-3D-Printing)
- [S] Material-dependent blog figures: PLA 55–60°, PETG 45–50°, ABS 40–45° maximum unsupported angle — [eufymake blog](https://www.eufymake.com/ca/blogs/printing-guides/3d-printing-overhang) (low-authority vendor blog)
- [S] Prusa forum: 45° doable on any printer; MINI/MK3S can push ~55° — [Prusa forum](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/how-do-i-print-this-printing-help/general-advice-on-adding-supports)

**Bridging**
- [P] Hubs: unless the bridge is "less than 5mm", sagging or support marks will be present; split the design or post-process for longer bridges — [Hubs FDM design](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-fdm-3d-printing/)
- [P] Xometry: "Horizontal bridge, max unsupported length 10 mm" — [Xometry FDM guide](https://xometry.pro/en/?p=154421)
- [P] Prusa: "Short horizontal bridges can be printed without supports" (no number given) — [Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)
- [S] Bambu TDS: 30 mm (PLA Basic, PET-CF) to 40 mm (engineering filaments) — see TDS links above
- [S] Student lab test on a Bambu P1S: unsupported horizontal overhangs show pronounced strands at ~6–7 mm; 10 mm "feasible but very messy" — [MIT 863.25 week 3](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/863.25/Architecture/week-3.html)

**Minimum wall thickness**
- [P] Prusa, 0.4 mm nozzle: 1 perimeter 0.45 mm, 2 → 0.9 mm, 3 → 1.35 mm, 4 → 1.8 mm; "Walls thinner than one nozzle perimeter are not printable"; walls between one and two perimeters are also marked not printable; "Detect thin walls" cannot repair sub-perimeter walls — [Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)
- [S] Prusa, Layers and perimeters: PrusaSlicer reports an optimal thin-wall value for a given perimeter count and layer height, e.g. two perimeters at 0.2 mm layer height = 0.86 mm rather than 0.9 mm — [Prusa KB, Layers and perimeters](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/layers-and-perimeters_1748) (snippet)
- [P] Hubs: "All 3D printers can successfully print components with wall thicknesses greater than 0.8 mm" — [Hubs, Key design considerations](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/key-design-considerations-3d-printing/)
- [P] Xometry: min wall supported 0.75 mm (recommended 1.5 mm); unsupported 1 mm (1.5 mm); min gap between walls 0.5 mm (1 mm) — [Xometry](https://xometry.pro/en/?p=154421)
- [P] JLC3DP (page "Last updated on Nov 04, 2026"): FDM wall 1.6 mm at 50×50 mm, 2.0 mm at 100×100 mm, 2.5 mm at 200×200 mm; >1.5 mm for protrusions, snaps, fasteners — [JLC3DP design guideline](https://jlc3dp.com/help/article/3d-printing-design-guideline)
- [S] JLC3DP blog: "1.2mm for structural walls and 0.8mm for non-structural features" — [JLC3DP blog](https://jlc3dp.com/blog/how-to-create-3d-models-for-printing)
- [S] Stratasys Direct: minimum wall ≥ 4× slice thickness (0.010" slice → 0.040" wall) — [Stratasys Direct FDM design guide PDF](https://info.stratasysdirect.com/rs/626-SBR-192/images/FDM_Design_Guide_Stratasys_Direct.pdf)
- [P] Meshy (vendor guidance for its own output): FDM minimum reliable wall 1.2 mm — [Meshy docs, 3D printing workflow](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/use-cases/3d-printing)

**Minimum feature / pin diameter**
- [P] Hubs: pins >5 mm are printed with perimeters and infill and are strong; pins <5 mm may be weak or fail; add a fillet at the base or drill and insert an off-the-shelf pin — [Hubs FDM design](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-fdm-3d-printing/)
- [P] Xometry: min feature 1.2 mm (1.5 mm recommended); min shaft/pin 1.2 mm (≥1.5 mm) at 1 mm height — [Xometry](https://xometry.pro/en/?p=154421)
- [P] JLC3DP: pin 2.0 mm (height 2–4 mm), 3.0 mm (height 3–6 mm) — [JLC3DP](https://jlc3dp.com/help/article/3d-printing-design-guideline)

**Embossed / engraved text**
- [P] Xometry: min embossed text height 0.50 mm; debossed depth 0.5 mm; min font (character height) 3.5 mm (5 mm recommended); shallower than 0.5 mm "may be unclear" — [Xometry](https://xometry.pro/en/?p=154421)
- [P] JLC3DP: embossed and engraved detail min 1.0 mm deep × 1.0 mm wide — [JLC3DP](https://jlc3dp.com/help/article/3d-printing-design-guideline)
- [S] HLH Rapid: emboss ≥0.3 mm high; engraving ≥0.5 mm deep and wide — [HLH Rapid](https://hlhrapid.com/knowledge/design-guide-fdm-3d-printing/); Wevolver rule of thumb 0.8 mm stroke, 0.5 mm height — [Wevolver](https://www.wevolver.com/article/fdm-3d-printing-design-tips-best-practices-and-techniques-for-stronger-prints); Materialise ABS: line ≥2.5 mm, depth ≥0.5 mm — [Materialise](https://materialise.com/en/academy/industrial/design-am/abs)

**Holes and shrinkage compensation**
- [P] Hubs: vertical-axis holes print undersized; amount varies by printer, software, size and material; smaller holes affected more; print undersized and drill for critical diameters; rotating 90° so the hole axis is vertical removes the support need — [Hubs FDM design](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-fdm-3d-printing/)
- [P] JLC3DP: min hole 1.5 mm (depth 1.5–4.5 mm), 2.0 mm (depth 2–6 mm); hole tolerance ±0.4 mm; "holes generally shrink" — [JLC3DP](https://jlc3dp.com/help/article/3d-printing-design-guideline); [S] another JLC3DP page: design holes 0.1–0.2 mm larger than nominal — [JLC3DP blog](https://jlc3dp.com/blog/how-to-design-3d-models-for-printing)
- [P] Xometry: min hole 1 mm at 1 mm thickness — [Xometry](https://xometry.pro/en/?p=154421)
- [S] PrusaSlicer "XY Size Compensation" is for small adjustments (threads/holes), positive enlarges, negative shrinks; no official number — [Prusa KB per-model settings thread](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/per-model-settings_1674)

**Clearances and fits**
- [P] Prusa: "An Original Prusa will be accurate to at least 0.2 mm"; "An initial good measurement for movable parts is at least 0.3 mm"; no universal tolerance — [Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)
- [P] Xometry: min clearance for moving parts 0.5 mm; clearances under 0.5 mm may fuse; general tolerance ±0.5% (±0.5 mm) — [Xometry](https://xometry.pro/en/?p=154421)
- [P] JLC3DP: assembled-part clearance 0.5 mm; moving-part clearance 0.5 mm; model tolerance ±0.3 mm within 100 mm, ±0.4% above — [JLC3DP](https://jlc3dp.com/help/article/3d-printing-design-guideline); [S] other JLC3DP pages say 0.2 mm for mating parts and 0.2–0.5 mm assembly range — [JLC3DP blog](https://jlc3dp.com/blog/how-to-design-3d-models-for-printing)
- [P] Hubs dimensional accuracy: desktop FDM ±0.5% with ±0.5 mm lower limit; industrial FDM ±0.15% with ±0.2 mm lower limit — [Hubs, Dimensional accuracy](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/dimensional-accuracy-3d-printed-parts/)
- [S] Press-fit conventions conflict: Zbotic lists interference (shaft larger than hole) 0.1–0.2 mm hand press, 0.2–0.4 mm mallet, 0.4–0.6 mm vice — [Zbotic](https://zbotic.in/3d-printing-tolerances-designing-gaps-for-press-fits-threads-and-snap-fits/); Leyaa lists press fit 0.0–0.1 mm clearance, sliding 0.1–0.3 mm — [Leyaa](https://leyaa.ai/core-engineering/learn/3d-printing/qna/how-to-design-tolerance-for-3d-printing); Sovol warns nominal 0.1 mm press ends up far tighter in high-shrink ASA and recommends labelled test coupons — [Sovol](https://www.sovol3d.com/blogs/news/fdm-3d-printing-tolerances-clearances-how-to-design-parts-that-fit)

**Chamfer vs fillet at the bed, elephant foot**
- [P] Hubs: add a "45° chamfer or radius" on all edges touching the build plate to reduce elephant's foot and warping and ease removal; internal corners have a radius equal to the nozzle size — [Hubs FDM design](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-fdm-3d-printing/)
- [P] Prusa: fillets on the face toward the bed create a steep overhang that hurts surface quality; "use chamfers when a perfect finish matters"; variable layer height helps fillets; chamfers on slot edges ease assembly — [Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)
- [P] Prusa elephant-foot compensation: values "around 0.2 mm usually work well" for the default 0.4 mm nozzle; on in official Prusa profiles; PrusaSlicer 2.2+ avoids over-shrinking thin first-layer lines; too-high values weaken brim attachment — [Prusa KB, Elephant foot compensation](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/elephant-foot-compensation_114487)
- [S] Markforged Onyx GF: fillets on the bottom face introduce low-angle overhangs (<40° to the bed) that can cause warping — [Markforged, Onyx GF best practices](https://support.markforged.com/portal/s/article/Best-Practices-for-Designing-Onyx-GF-Parts)

**Warping risk by footprint and material**
- [P] Hubs: shrinkage typically "0.2 - 1% range depending on the material"; higher-temperature materials warp more; large parts most affected (uneven cooling); add bottom-edge radius or brim; avoid large flat surfaces, add ribs, use fillets at internal corners — [Hubs, Dimensional accuracy](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/dimensional-accuracy-3d-printed-parts/), [Hubs, Key design considerations](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/key-design-considerations-3d-printing/)
- [S] Cooling shrinkage table: PLA 0.3–0.5%, PETG 0.2–1.0%, ASA 0.4–0.7%, ABS 0.7–1.6%, Nylon 12 0.7–2.0%, Nylon 6-6 0.7–3.0%; shrinkage is a percentage of part size — [Filament2Print](https://filament2print.com/en/blog/contrazioni-warping-stampa-3d); 3DSourced gives wider ranges (PLA 0.2–3%, ABS up to 11%) — [3DSourced](https://www.3dsourced.com/guides/3d-print-shrinkage-pla-abs-petg/)

**Layer-adhesion anisotropy (where parts snap)**
- [P] Prusa: "Like wood, FFF prints are stronger in one direction than another", so orient structural parts accordingly; surfaces printed over supports are rougher — [Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)
- [S] Thesis (100% infill, 0.2 mm layers): X-direction specimens stronger than Z by 24.63% (PLA), 42% (ABS), 127.9% (PETG) due to inter-layer adhesion, i.e. PLA Z ≈ 80% of XY — [Sacramento State thesis](https://scholars.csus.edu/esploro/outputs/graduate/Mechanical-and-FEA-comparison-of-FDM/99258311232901671)
- [S] Formlabs blog cites an FDM experiment where Z-axis yield strength was ~55% of X — [Formlabs](https://formlabs.com/global/blog/isotropy-in-SLA-3D-printing/); MIT Fab Academy PLA tests: Z specimens delaminated at 50% (Makerbot) and 75% (Sindoh) of XY load; modulus similar across orientations — [MIT Fab Academy](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/863.16/section.Harvard/people/Garbarini/week3.html); review paper: Z strength "sometimes by an order of magnitude" lower — [PMC6414919](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6414919)

**Flexible (TPU) minimum thickness**
- [S] FDM-specific figure: "Flexible materials like TPU require a higher minimum of around 2.0 mm" vs 0.8 mm PLA — [Flsun blog](https://flsun3d.com/blogs/knowledge/what-do-you-know-about-wall-thickness-in-3d-printing) (vendor blog)
- [S] SLS TPU figures (not FDM): Raise3D RMS220 TPU guide 0.6 mm minimum — [Raise3D wiki](https://wiki.raise3d.com/en/RMS220Series/RMS220printer/TPUDesignGuide); Sculpteo TPU 88A 0.8 mm — [Sculpteo](https://pro.sculpteo.com/en/materials/sls-material/tpu88a/); Materialise TPU 90A ≥1 mm — [Materialise](https://materialise.com/en/academy/industrial/design-am/tpu-90a)

**Material detail rendering**
- [P] Prusa: PC, ASA, PETG and similar "may not render small details as well as PLA" — [Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)

### Inferences
- Suggested encodable defaults (conservative / tuned-printer), with attribution: overhang limit 45° (Hubs, Xometry) / 55–60° (Bambu PLA TDS, Prusa) and 75° only for Nextruder-class cooling (Prusa); bridge 5 mm (Hubs) / 10 mm (Xometry) / 30 mm (Bambu PLA TDS); wall minimum 0.9 mm = two perimeters at 0.4 mm (Prusa) with 0.45 mm as an absolute floor; "service-bureau safe" wall 1.2–1.6 mm (JLC3DP, Meshy, Xometry recommended); pin/feature ≥1.5 mm (Xometry) and ≥2 mm for load-bearing (JLC3DP); emboss ≥0.5 mm high with stroke ≥0.8 mm (Xometry, Wevolver); hole ≥1.5–2 mm, expect −0.2 to −0.4 mm (JLC3DP ±0.4 mm tolerance); clearance 0.3 mm (Prusa, tuned) / 0.5 mm (Xometry, JLC3DP) for moving parts; elephant-foot compensation 0.2 mm or a 0.4–0.5 mm 45° chamfer at the bed edge (Prusa, Hubs).
- The overhang limit's dependence on layer height follows from geometry: the horizontal step per layer equals layer_height × tan(angle from vertical), and Hubs' "~50% overlap at 45°" statement implies overlap fraction ≈ 1 − (layer_height × tan θ)/extrusion_width; thinner layers therefore raise the printable angle at fixed line width. This derivation is mine, not stated by a source.
- Z-direction tensile strength spans roughly 45–80% of XY across the cited tests, so a "snaps here" check should flag any load-bearing cross-section whose thinnest dimension lies along Z and apply at least a 2× knock-down relative to XY design strength.
- Service bureaus (Xometry, JLC3DP) publish larger minimums than printer makers (Prusa) because they must guarantee results across machines; for a personal tuned printer the Prusa figures are usable, for shareable models the bureau figures are safer.

### Gaps
- No official Bambu Lab wiki "design guidelines" page was found; Bambu numbers exist only in per-filament TDS PDFs (direct fetch blocked).
- Formlabs publishes no FDM design guide (its guides are SLA/SLS); Markforged's "Designing for plastic 3D printing" page returned HTTP 401.
- No peer-reviewed study was found quantifying hole shrinkage versus diameter for consumer FDM; the All3DP tolerance-test article returned 403.
- No authoritative FDM TPU minimum-thickness figure from a filament maker or printer maker was found; only a vendor blog (2.0 mm).
- Warping risk as a function of footprint area is described qualitatively everywhere; no source gives a numeric footprint threshold.

## Part A, Q2: Orientation optimization in code (Tweaker-3, OrcaSlicer/Bambu Studio auto-orient, PrusaSlicer, academic, trimesh)

### Takeaway
Tweaker-3 (GPL-3.0, pure numpy, maintained, pushed 2026-03-24) is the only scriptable open-source FDM auto-orienter; OrcaSlicer/Bambu Studio's auto-orient is a C++ port using Tweaker-3's exact trained constants and is reachable headless through the slicer CLI `--orient`; PrusaSlicer still has no FDM auto-orient (issue open since 2024). Objective functions in the wild combine overhang area, bottom contact area, bottom contour length and, in Orca, near-horizontal faces and a bottom-area stability penalty.

### Cited Findings

**Tweaker-3 (ChristophSchranz)**
- [P] Repo: "A performant auto-rotate software for objects in 3D Printing, whose parameter are trained by an evolutionary algorithm"; license GPL-3.0; 110 stars; last push 2026-03-24; not archived — [GitHub API / repo](https://github.com/ChristophSchranz/Tweaker-3)
- [P] Only runtime dependency is `numpy>=1.16` (requirements.txt); build via setuptools/setuptools_scm (pyproject.toml) — [requirements.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChristophSchranz/Tweaker-3/master/requirements.txt), [pyproject.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChristophSchranz/Tweaker-3/master/pyproject.toml)
- [P] README: input STL and 3MF; output STL (binary or ASCII), "3MF output not supported yet"; CLI `python Tweaker.py -i file.stl -vb`; `-x` extended mode (more reliable, slower, "suggested"); `-min sur` minimizes support surface instead of default support volume; `-c` convert only, `-fs` favour a side, `-r` results only, `-p` progress; pip-installable CLI `tweaker3`; "maintained and continuously improved since 2017 in my spare time"; original author date 12.01.2016, Salzburg Research; integrated in Cura as "Auto-Orientation" marketplace plugin (nallath/CuraOrientationPlugin, Cura ≥4) and OctoPrint PrePrint-Service; whitepaper "Tweaker - Auto Rotation Module for FDM 3D Printing" on ResearchGate — [Tweaker-3 README](https://github.com/ChristophSchranz/Tweaker-3), [whitepaper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/311765131_Tweaker_-_Auto_Rotation_Module_for_FDM_3D_Printing)
- [P] Algorithm from MeshTweaker.py: candidate orientations come from `area_cumulation` ("accumulation of the magnitude of parallel area vectors"), `death_star` ("Creating random faces by adding a random vertex to an existing edge. Common orientations of these faces are promising orientations"), and `add_supplements` (18 fixed vectors: ±axes and 45° diagonals), then `remove_duplicates`. Score: `target_function(bottom, overhang, contour, min_volume)` returns an "Unprintability" = `TAR_A*(overhang+TAR_B) + RELATIVE_F*(overhang+TAR_C)/(TAR_D + CONTOUR_F*contour + BOTTOM_F*bottom [+ TAR_E*overhang])`; in min-volume mode `overhang /= 25` ("a volume is of higher dimension"). Two trained parameter sets exist, e.g. the volume set RELATIVE_F 6.610621027964314, CONTOUR_F 0.23228623269775997, BOTTOM_F 1.167152017941474, TAR_C 0.24308070476924726, TAR_D 0.6284515508160871, ASCENT −0.2389…, NEGL_FACE_SIZE 0.4929 — [MeshTweaker.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChristophSchranz/Tweaker-3/master/MeshTweaker.py)
- [P] Predecessor iot-salzburg/STL-tweaker: LGPL-3.0, 29 stars, last push 2022-11-20 — [GitHub API](https://github.com/iot-salzburg/STL-tweaker)

**OrcaSlicer / Bambu Studio auto-orient**
- [P] `src/libslic3r/Orient.hpp` `struct OrientParams` defaults: TAR_A 0.01, TAR_B 0.177, RELATIVE_F 6.610621027964314, CONTOUR_F 0.23228623269775997, BOTTOM_F 1.167152017941474, TAR_C 0.24308070476924726, TAR_D 0.6284515508160871, TAR_E 0, FIRST_LAY_H 0.2, ASCENT −0.5, LAF_MAX 0.999 (cos 1.4°), LAF_MIN 0.9703 (cos 14°), TAR_LAF 0.01, TAR_PROJ_AREA 0.1, BOTTOM_MIN 0.1 ("min bottom area. If lower than it the objects may be unstable"), overhang_angle 60 (OrientMesh default 30), min_volume false — [Orient.hpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/main/src/libslic3r/Orient.hpp)
- [P] `Orient.cpp` `target_function`: with min_volume, `overhang = costs.overhang/25; cost = TAR_A*(overhang+TAR_B) + RELATIVE_F*(overhang*TAR_C + TAR_D + TAR_LAF*area_laf*use_low_angle_face) / (TAR_D + CONTOUR_F*contour + BOTTOM_F*bottom + BOTTOM_HULL_F*bottom_hull + TAR_E*overhang + TAR_PROJ_AREA*area_projected)`; otherwise same without the TAR_A term; then `cost += (costs.bottom < params.BOTTOM_MIN) * 100`; lowest "unprintability" wins. Candidates: `area_cumulation_accurate` top 10 quantized face-normal groups plus top 14 from the convex hull, plus `add_supplements()` fixed directions; overhang area counts faces whose normal projection is below `ASCENT = cos(PI − overhang_angle·PI/180)`, height-weighted in min-volume mode; bottom area = faces below first-layer height; contour approximated as `4*sqrt(bottom)`; near-tie prefers upright to avoid flipping; a cooling-direction pass samples 180 XY directions — [Orient.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/main/src/libslic3r/Orient.cpp)
- [P] Orient.cpp contains no comment referencing Tweaker or Schranz (grep for "tweaker|schranz" returned nothing; header has no attribution) — [Orient.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/main/src/libslic3r/Orient.cpp)
- [P] CLI: `PrintConfig.cpp` defines option `orient` (coInt), label "Orient Options", tooltip "Orient options: 0-disable, 1-enable, others-auto", default 0; a `repair` (coBool) option follows it — [PrintConfig.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/main/src/libslic3r/PrintConfig.cpp)
- [P] Bambu Studio GitHub wiki "Command Line Usage" lists `--orient` "Orient the model", `--arrange` "0-disable, 1-enable, others-auto", `--slice plate_index`, `--export-3mf`; examples invoke the `./bambu-studio` binary; no explicit "headless" section — [BambuStudio wiki](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/wiki/Command-Line-Usage)
- [P] idea-to-print drives a local Bambu Studio install "through an offline CLI launcher for Windows or macOS" to slice headlessly — [idea-to-print README](https://github.com/Rjxshr1/idea-to-print/blob/main/README.en.md)

**Academic**
- [S] Ezair, Massarwi, Elber, "Orientation analysis of 3D objects toward minimal support volume in 3D-printing", Computers & Graphics 2015: support volume is "a continuous but non-smooth function of the orientation angles"; GPU-capable algorithm computes support volume per orientation and finds a minimum that is "for practical purposes, in many cases optimal" — [AMiner record](https://aminer.org/pub/5736963d6e3b12023e5490d9/orientation-analysis-of-d-objects-toward-minimal-support-volume-in-d-printing), [Technion CRIS](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/orientation-analysis-of-3d-objects-toward-minimal-support-volume-/)
- [S] Umetani & Schmidt, "Cross-sectional structural analysis for 3D printing optimization" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2013 Technical Briefs): avoids volumetric meshes and linear solves for interactive speed; optimizes orientation to increase mechanical strength; Autodesk patents US10239258 / US10759118 describe slicing into 2D cross-sections, computing stresses, translating to weakness metrics, choosing the orientation minimizing weakness — [paper PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/cross-sectional-structural-analysis-for-3d-printing-kyq3lpp4jf.pdf), [US10759118](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10759118)
- [S] Zhang, Le, Panotopoulou, Whiting, Wang, "Perceptual models of preference in 3D printing direction", ACM TOG 34(6) 2015: learned preference model combining support area, visual saliency, preferred viewpoint and smoothness preservation, fitted from a large-scale user study — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~athina/papers/zhang2015perceptual.pdf), [DBLP](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/rec/journals/tog/ZhangLPWW15.html)
- [S] Related patent "Orientation optimization in 3D printing" US10571893 — [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10571893)

**Python/trimesh building blocks**
- [P] trimesh: MIT; 3,719 stars; pushed 2026-11-06; features include `is_watertight`, repair of winding/normals/holes, ray-mesh queries (fast path via `embreex`), convex hull, mass properties (volume, center of mass, inertia), booleans via Manifold3D or Blender, voxelization, cross-sections and "slicing for 3D-printing"; `pip install trimesh[easy]` pulls binary soft deps on Linux x86_64, **macOS ARM** and Windows x86_64 — [trimesh repo](https://github.com/mikedh/trimesh)
- [P] A shipped example of the trimesh-style approach: text-to-cad's `cad stl dfm --process printing --angle-limit 45` reports down-facing area below the limit (excluding faces resting on the plate), an angle histogram, wall thickness via 2000 random surface rays (min/p05/median), a "prism upper-bound" support volume ("Ratios above ~30% of part volume usually justify reorientation"), watertightness, body count and internal voids, and with `--candidates` ranks the six axis-aligned orientations — [text-to-cad manufacturing-printing.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/main/skills/cad/references/manufacturing-printing.md)
- [S] PySLM (metal AM) performs overhang extraction and projection-based support volumes on trimesh meshes (see Q3) — [PySLM docs](https://pyslm.readthedocs.io/en/dev/examples/basic_support.html)

### Inferences
- OrcaSlicer/Bambu Studio's auto-orient is a port of Tweaker-3: five trained constants (RELATIVE_F, CONTOUR_F, BOTTOM_F, TAR_C, TAR_D) match Tweaker-3's volume parameter set to 16 significant digits and the candidate-generation steps (`area_cumulation`, `add_supplements`) share names; the port omits `death_star` sampling, adds convex-hull candidates, a low-angle-face term and the `BOTTOM_MIN` stability penalty. Since Tweaker-3 is GPL-3.0 and OrcaSlicer is AGPL-3.0 (PrusaSlicer lineage; license not re-verified this session), the missing attribution comment is a provenance gap worth noting, not a usability blocker.
- For a deterministic Python CLI on Apple Silicon, Tweaker-3 is the direct fit: numpy-only, no GPU, STL/3MF in, STL out; wrap it to emit the chosen rotation matrix and the two cost terms (overhang area, bottom area) as your own metrics rather than the opaque "unprintability" scalar.
- Headless orientation through OrcaSlicer/Bambu Studio CLI (`--orient 1`) is possible on macOS but couples you to a GUI app binary; use it as a cross-check, not the primary path.
- A minimal trimesh overhang metric: per face, `cos_down = dot(normal, (0,0,-1))`; overhang if `cos_down > cos(90° − θ_limit)` and face centroid z > z_min + first_layer; overhang area = Σ face areas; support-volume upper bound = Σ area × (centroid_z − z_floor) × |cos_down| where z_floor comes from a downward ray hit or the bed. This mirrors Orca's height-weighted overhang term and text-to-cad's prism estimate.

- OrcaSlicer's own license field was not returned by the GitHub API in this session (rate-limited); AGPL-3.0 is assumed from PrusaSlicer/Bambu Studio lineage.
- No document states whether `--orient` in OrcaSlicer/Bambu Studio CLI runs without a display server on macOS; idea-to-print's "offline CLI launcher" is indirect evidence that it does.
- Tweaker-3's whitepaper (ResearchGate) was not fetched; the evolutionary-training details and reported accuracy are unverified.
- The Ezair 2015 method's internals (sampling strategy, convex-hull use) were not confirmed beyond the abstract.

## Part A, Q3: Support generation, support-free design, and estimating support volume without a slicer

### Takeaway
Tree/organic supports are now standard in PrusaSlicer (since 2.6, 2023), Cura and Bambu Studio/Orca, and PrusaSlicer 2.6 also auto-detects where supports are needed; academic "support slimming" deforms meshes toward self-supporting shapes but has no maintained open-source implementation found. No pure-Python FDM support-volume library exists; the closest are PySLM (metal AM, LGPL-2.1) and text-to-cad's prism upper bound (MIT).

### Cited Findings
- [P] PrusaSlicer 2.6 (June 19 2023, older): Organic supports are "an evolution of tree supports by Thomas Rahm, which were in turn improved from Cura's tree supports"; branches are smarter, shorter, straighter, with a circular cross-section and automatic double perimeters; an "Automatic support painter" analyses extrusions (bridging, supported weight) and warns at slice time if supports are needed; "Dynamic overhang speed" and "Avoid crossing curled overhangs" were added; PrusaSlicer first did tree supports for SLA in 2.0 (2019) — [Prusa blog, PrusaSlicer 2.6](https://blog.prusa3d.com/prusaslicer-2-6-is-here-organic-supports-text-embossing-new-cut-tool-and-more_79322/)
- [P] PrusaSlicer paint-on supports and hollowing articles exist in the KB — [Paint-on supports](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/paint-on-supports_168584)
- [S] Hu, Jin, Wang, "Support slimming for single material based additive manufacturing", Computer-Aided Design 2015: encloses the model in a volumetric mesh and applies an as-rigid-as-possible deformation to make it more self-supporting, with control over deformation and search for printing directions — [paper PDF](https://mewangcl.github.io/pubs/CADOrientOptm.pdf); follow-up Hu, Zhang, Wang (CASE 2015) computes the minimal rotation via Gauss-sphere analysis — [CASE15 PDF](https://mewangcl.github.io/pubs/CASE15OrientOptm.pdf)
- [S] A TU Delft thesis treats "support-free hollowing" (interior voids with self-supporting surfaces via planar offsetting and shape optimization) — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_3f45ff50-dbb7-4c43-b071-16b0e4e1eb81?preview=1); a US patent (12430852) covers mesh modification to remove overhangs while keeping watertightness and limiting volume change — [USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12430852)
- [P] PySLM (drlukeparry/pyslm): LGPL-2.1; 198 stars; pip `PythonSLM`; built on trimesh 4 and Clipper2 (pyclipr); features "overhang surface extraction", projection-based overhang analysis (GPU GLSL ray tracing), block support generation by projection, "exact support volumes using the Manifold CSG library"; targets SLM/DMLS/SLS and bitmap SLA/DLP, FDM not mentioned — [PySLM repo](https://github.com/drlukeparry/pyslm), [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/drlukeparry/pyslm/master/README.rst)
- [P] text-to-cad (MIT): "Support volume is a prism upper-bound estimate for cost and post-processing effort, not a slicer-accurate figure"; min hole, min positive feature and max bridge "have no measured counterpart yet" — [manufacturing-printing.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/main/skills/cad/references/manufacturing-printing.md)
- [S] A web calculator describes its estimate as "an approximation from overhang footprint and drop height" — [iamrapid](https://iamrapid.com/tools/support-estimator-overhang-analyzer); an academic review notes accurate support volume requires replicating the full process for each orientation — [UniBo PDF](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/f934083e-87bf-45a7-a15e-2f8bbd913f70/Efficient%20part%20orientation%20algorithm%20for%20additive%20manufacturing%20in%20industrial%20applications.pdf)
- [P] Meshy's "Printability" card reports Watertight, Holes and Non-manifold edges, and "Make Printable" (10 credits, paid plan) fills holes/open boundaries and thickens thin walls — [Meshy docs](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/use-cases/3d-printing)

### Inferences
- The cheapest deterministic support-need detector is the overhang-face classifier from Q2 plus connected-component grouping of overhang faces (trimesh `split`/face adjacency) to produce "support islands" with area, drop height and an area×height volume bound; PySLM's projection approach is the same idea with exact CSG volumes, and could be reused for FDM because the geometry step is process-agnostic even though the package targets metal.
- For a slicer-accurate number, run PrusaSlicer or OrcaSlicer CLI with and without supports and diff reported filament usage; this is heavier but it is the only "ground truth" available (not verified in this session that CLI output exposes support filament separately).
- Support-free deformation (Hu et al.) is not available as a maintained library; for generated figurines, the practical equivalents are adding a base (Meshy, PrintU, idea-to-print all do this) and re-orienting.

### Gaps
- No source fetched for Bambu Studio's or Cura's current tree-support algorithm parameters (search budget exhausted).
- No open-source implementation of support slimming or support-free hollowing was located.
- Whether PrusaSlicer/OrcaSlicer CLI reports support-material volume separately in headless mode was not verified.

## Part A, Q4: Automatable structural sanity checks (thin necks, center of mass/tipping, bed-contact ratio, minimum cross-section along Z)

### Takeaway
The research lineage is clear (Stress Relief 2012, Worst-case 2013, Make It Stand 2013, cross-sectional analysis 2013) but none ships as a maintained library; practical checks today are geometric proxies that at least two open-source pipelines already implement: ray-cast wall thickness, pairwise self-intersection, bed-contact area, and center-of-mass projection against the contact footprint.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Stava et al., "Stress Relief: improving structural strength of 3D printable objects" (ACM TOG 2012): detection combines a lightweight structural solver with 3D medial-axis approximations; correction uses hollowing, thickening and strut insertion, iterated until problems are eliminated — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Stava12ToG.pdf), [SIGGRAPH archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=110527)
- [S] Zhou, Panetta, Zorin, "Worst-case structural analysis" (SIGGRAPH 2013): finds structural problems "based on geometry and material properties only, without specific assumptions on loads", via constrained optimization for the worst load distribution — [NYU PDF](https://cims.nyu.edu/~dzorin/papers/zhou2013wcs.pdf); Langlois et al. 2016 note it can mispredict interior struts as weak — [MIT PDF](https://cfg.mit.edu/assets/files/stochastic.pdf)
- [S] Prévost, Whiting, Lefebvre, Sorkine-Hornung, "Make It Stand" (SIGGRAPH 2013): printed models "topple instead of standing as initially intended"; voxelizes the object, carves interior and deforms exterior to move the center of mass; extended to multiple bases; open reimplementation MultiW/make-it-balance; 2016 follow-up uses movable steel balls — [HAL record](https://hal-univ-tlse3.archives-ouvertes.fr/INRIA/hal-00926855v1), [make-it-balance](https://github.com/MultiW/make-it-balance), [Prévost 2016](https://cs.dartmouth.edu/wjarosz/publications/prevost16balancing.pdf)
- [S] Umetani & Schmidt cross-sectional analysis: slice the model into 2D cross-sections, compute stresses per section, convert to weakness metrics — [US10759118](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10759118)
- [P] OrcaSlicer auto-orient penalizes orientations with bottom area below `BOTTOM_MIN = 0.1` ("the objects may be unstable") by +100 — [Orient.hpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/main/src/libslic3r/Orient.hpp), [Orient.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SoftFever/OrcaSlicer/main/src/libslic3r/Orient.cpp)
- [P] idea-to-print's built-in geometry checks: pairwise self-intersection for every face pair; per-face wall, detail and connection thickness once thresholds are configured; "base contact area and centre-of-mass stability", computed for both solid and hollow-shell mass models with the worst reported; checks without thresholds stay `UNKNOWN`; `print_ready` requires all geometry checks to pass plus appearance and slice review; the demo runs 16 geometry checks — [idea-to-print README](https://github.com/Rjxshr1/idea-to-print/blob/main/README.en.md)
- [P] text-to-cad's `dfm` wall-thickness field: rays from random surface points inward along the normal, counted when the exit face is turned ≥120° from the entry face ("a wall's other side, or a wedge's of up to 60 degrees, never a corner's"); reports `min_mm`, `p05_mm`, median; per-body breakdown so fit gaps are not recorded as walls; recommends `--samples 20000` before a print — [manufacturing-printing.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/main/skills/cad/references/manufacturing-printing.md)
- [P] trimesh exposes center of mass, volume, inertia, convex hull and cross-sections, the primitives needed for these checks — [trimesh](https://github.com/mikedh/trimesh)
- [P] Hubs: pins under 5 mm may fail; add a base fillet — [Hubs FDM](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-parts-fdm-3d-printing/)

### Inferences
- Thin-neck detection: slice the mesh at fine Z steps (trimesh `section`), compute per-slice total area and per-island area; flag any island whose area or minimum width (inscribed circle via polygon buffering) drops below the pin rule (≥1.5–2 mm diameter) or whose area ratio to the slice above/below drops sharply; this is the 2D analogue of Umetani's cross-sectional analysis and of Stava's medial-axis thinness.
- Tipping: compute center of mass (trimesh, assume uniform density or the slicer's shell+infill density), project onto the bed plane, and require it to fall inside the convex hull of the bed-contact polygon with a margin; report contact-area / footprint-hull-area and contact-area / max-cross-section-area ratios (Orca's BOTTOM_MIN is a crude version of this).
- Anisotropy hot spots: combine the thin-neck pass with the Z-strength knock-down from Q1 so a neck aligned with Z is flagged at ~2× the XY threshold.

### Gaps
- No maintained Python package implementing worst-case or stress-relief analysis was found; make-it-balance is a student reimplementation of unknown state (not fetched).
- No source gives validated numeric thresholds for "will tip" margins or contact-area ratios for FDM; the ≥3 mm base (Meshy) and BOTTOM_MIN (Orca) are the only encoded values found.

A cloud API (Hunyuan3D 3.1 as idea-to-print does, or Meshy) is the realistic path for quality, with local deterministic post-processing.
- The strongest deterministic, scriptable, open-license stack assembled from verified pieces is: generation (API or TripoSR) → trimesh (MIT) + manifold3d (Apache-2.0) for watertight/boolean/base-merge → optional voxel/dual-contouring remesh (ComfyUI node logic) → Tweaker-3 (GPL-3.0) for orientation → text-to-cad-style `dfm` metrics or your own trimesh checks → OrcaSlicer/Bambu Studio CLI for slicing. pymeshfix is GPL plus a non-commercial MeshFix restriction, so avoid it in a redistributable tool.

## Part B, Q4: What the community has learned about AI-generated models for printing (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
The recurring defects are non-manifold edges and holes, cracks/self-intersections, excessive triangle density, thin or floating features, speculative back sides, and cover renders that do not match prints; the recurring fixes are voxel/dual-contouring remesh, automated repair (Meshy "Make Printable", MeshFix/manifold), adding a thick base, and demanding real print photos. Platforms responded with labels and filters (Printables, Thingiverse, MakerWorld AIGC) and points changes; no source reports measured success rates.

### Inferences
- The community's consensus checklist for AI meshes maps directly onto automatable gates: (1) watertight and 2-manifold (trimesh/manifold), (2) single connected body or deliberately split parts, (3) triangle budget (decimate to ~100k), (4) minimum wall/feature thickness at the target scale (ray-cast), (5) a base of ≥3 mm with COM inside the footprint, (6) overhang/support estimate and orientation, (7) a real print photo before publishing.
- "Slop" complaints are about volume and misrepresentation more than geometry; a pipeline that publishes should enforce the AIGC label and real photos to avoid penalties.

- No source quantifies first-print success rates for any generator.
- No Hackaday article from 2025–2026 reviewing AI mesh generators for printing was found; coverage is older (2022–2023).
